# Who to follow in the AI conversation: a guide to the voices behind the year's commentary

*Sixteen people and groups account for much of what was written and quoted between November 2025 and November 2026. Here is what each one focuses on, where they usually land, and how you might use them in your own writing.*

*Period: 2025-11-11 to 2026-11-10 · 26,923 findings from 19 sources · generated 2026-11-14 by `python -m src.insights write`. Numbers and tables come from code; prose is Sonnet's; every link is a finding the text cites.*

## Key points

- Start with Simon Willison. He has the most findings of anyone (1,552), spread across devtools, coding agents and safety. He is the closest match to a builder who writes in public, and he is the easiest person to learn from and reply to. ([Simon Willison's Weblog, 2026-11-09](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/9/mysterious-beeps/)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-02-03](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/unless-that-claw-is-the-famous-openclaw)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-07-30](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-07-30-not-much/))
- Add Ben Tossell for people who build with AI tools for fun and for work. His material centres on coding agents and productivity, and it includes small personal projects like a reading-history timeline. ([Ben's Bites, 2026-11-05](https://www.bensbites.com/p/craft-and-taste)) ([Ben's Bites, 2026-11-03](https://www.bensbites.com/p/if-you-feel-behind-right-now-dont)) ([Ben's Bites, 2026-11-10](https://www.bensbites.com/p/how-i-got-seduced))
- Read Zvi Mowshowitz for safety, governance and evals, and Tomasz Tunguz for the business and cost angle. Together they cover the 'so what for companies' questions an MBA audience asks. ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-11-03](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/openai-updates-its-model-spec)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-10-28](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/anthropic-files-its-s-1)) ([Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-11-04](https://tomtunguz.com/watts-in-store-for-2027-inference/)) ([Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-11-06](https://tomtunguz.com/a-90-dollar-key-into-your-companys-secrets/))

## Simon Willison: the practical builder's reference

Willison has the largest footprint, with 1,552 findings. They concentrate on devtools and IDEs, coding agents, and safety and governance, and most of them are either a tool or technique or a pain point. He is quoted both on his own weblog and in Zvi Mowshowitz's roundups. ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-02-03](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/unless-that-claw-is-the-famous-openclaw)) ([Simon Willison's Weblog, 2026-11-09](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/9/mysterious-beeps/))

His typical stance is hands-on and cautious. He asks for a safer version of a powerful personal agent that keeps the useful parts but limits the attack surface. He also points out opaque details, such as the search providers hidden behind AI products. **Why follow:** he shows you how to test tools yourself and write down what you find. **Reply to him** with concrete things you built or tried. ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-02-03](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/unless-that-claw-is-the-famous-openclaw)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-07-30](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-07-30-not-much/))

## Ben Tossell: vibe coding for a general audience

Tossell (713 findings, via Ben's Bites) focuses on coding agents, agent orchestration and productivity. His findings are mostly tools and techniques, along with small build ideas. Examples include a Chrome extension that replaces clickbait thumbnails with real stills, and a personal reading-history timeline he built for fun. ([Ben's Bites, 2026-11-03](https://www.bensbites.com/p/if-you-feel-behind-right-now-dont)) ([Ben's Bites, 2026-11-05](https://www.bensbites.com/p/craft-and-taste))

He also notices how companies productize their internal tools; he cited Ramp's AI-driven spend tracking. **Why follow:** he is the closest peer to your own vibe-coding practice and a steady source of small, concrete examples to riff on. ([Ben's Bites, 2026-11-10](https://www.bensbites.com/p/how-i-got-seduced))

## Zvi Mowshowitz: the safety and governance digest

Mowshowitz (922 findings, via Don't Worry About the Vase) covers safety and governance, evals and testing, and consumer apps. Most of his findings are pain points, and he has a sizeable share of debates. His requests are specific. He wants agents to confirm before actions that affect third parties, a tool that separates boilerplate from substance in long filings like S-1s, and harder evals. ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-11-03](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/openai-updates-its-model-spec)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-10-28](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/anthropic-files-its-s-1)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-10-26](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/gemini-4-argon-the-model-and-safety))

His blog also relays many other voices, so he works as a hub. **Why follow:** you can follow most of the AI-risk debate through one source. **How to use him:** quote his concrete product ideas, such as the confirm-before-acting check, as examples for a broad audience. ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-11-03](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/openai-updates-its-model-spec)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-10-28](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/anthropic-files-its-s-1))

## Tomasz Tunguz: AI as a business and cost problem

Tunguz (833 findings) focuses on business and markets, agents and orchestration, and observability and cost. His ideas treat compute like an economic resource. One is a pricing and routing layer that sells inference by latency and bandwidth tiers, like internet access. Another is security tooling cheap enough to push expected attacker value below zero. ([Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-11-04](https://tomtunguz.com/watts-in-store-for-2027-inference/)) ([Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-11-06](https://tomtunguz.com/a-90-dollar-key-into-your-companys-secrets/))

He also reports on tools from a user's seat. He wrote that Claude Cowork was slow, crashed on startup twice, and changed authorization settings for his Claude Code installation. **Why follow:** he turns technical issues into business framing, which suits LinkedIn. ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-01-21](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-codes-3))

## Gary Marcus: the skeptic

Marcus (529 findings) writes on safety and governance, research and capabilities, and business and markets. His findings lean toward pain points and debates. He argues for systems that are safe by design and follow instructions reliably, rather than relying on next-word prediction alone. He also wants defensive tools against AI-driven phishing and deepfake fraud. ([Marcus on AI, 2026-10-31](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/report-from-the-vatican)) ([Marcus on AI, 2026-10-22](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/breaking-news-us-banks-are-taking))

On evals, he has argued that whether benchmarks saturate depends on whether you demand 80% or 95%+ reliability. **Why quote him:** he gives a clear counterweight to optimistic claims. **Why reply to him:** his reliability framing is easy to test against your own app experience. ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-05-13](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/cyber-lack-of-security-and-ai-governance))

## Open models and training: Nathan Lambert and Sebastian Raschka

Lambert (442 findings, Interconnects) focuses on model training and fine-tuning, local and open models, and research. He is cautious about evidence. For example, he flagged that RLVR results on Qwen models may be affected by data contamination and are hard to verify without open training data. He has also pointed to gaps in licensing for open-weight releases, and to the flood of vulnerability fixes as software gets produced faster than it is secured. ([Simon Willison's Weblog, 2025-11-22](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Nov/22/olmo-3/)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-07-30](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-179-part-1-a-louder-fire-alarm)) ([Interconnects, 2026-10-30](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/was-i-wrong-about-open-models-and))

Raschka (119 findings, Ahead of AI) is more technical: inference and infrastructure, training, and local models. His requests include tools that let LLMs operate graphical interfaces, and a router that picks reasoning effort automatically. **Why follow:** both help you judge open-versus-closed model claims without being an engineer. Raschka's count is smaller, but his topics are specific. ([Ahead of AI, 2026-09-09](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/gpt-6-astra-looped-transformers-and)) ([Ahead of AI, 2026-07-18](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/controlling-reasoning-effort-in-llms))

## Ethan Mollick and Alberto Romero: AI at work, explained for non-specialists

Mollick (185 findings, One Useful Thing) focuses on agents, productivity and knowledge work, and evals. He wants agents that can judge when their work is good enough, not merely whether it passes checks. He also wants deeper evaluations on hard real-world problems, since saturated public benchmarks and ELO scores drive conclusions that don't hold up. ([One Useful Thing, 2026-11-09](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/fable-52-and-jagged-agi)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-07-20](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/on-kimi-k3-its-capabilities-and-related))

Romero (298 findings, The Algorithmic Bridge) covers productivity, safety and consumer apps. His recent requests include tools to detect AI-written text and a translation layer that makes AI-generated math discoveries legible to human researchers. **Why follow both:** they write for a broad professional readership, which is your audience too. ([The Algorithmic Bridge, 2026-10-21](https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/put-some-brain-in-your-thinking)) ([The Algorithmic Bridge, 2026-10-07](https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/audience-at-a-magic-show)) ([The Algorithmic Bridge, 2026-09-12](https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/millennium-pastimes))

## Agent builders: swyx, Alessio Fanelli and omarsar0

swyx (174 findings) and swyx with Alessio Fanelli (106 findings), through Latent Space and AINews, focus on agents, coding agents and research. Their requests look ahead. They ask for a Git replacement that stores specs, traces and intent, and for agent harnesses that notice user friction in session transcripts and suggest fixes. They also want a record of why approaches failed, not just final results. ([Latent Space, 2026-11-08](https://latent.space/p/cs146s/)) ([Latent Space, 2026-10-21](https://latent.space/p/claude-mods/)) ([Latent Space, 2026-10-23](https://latent.space/p/ai-is-getting-good-at-math/))

omarsar0 (58 findings) stresses agent reliability. He says agents often fail because small off-path tool calls compound over long tasks, not because they lack capability. He also suggested a small dispatcher model to cut token waste in multi-agent systems. **Why follow:** they show what agent builders are asking for next. ([Latent Space, 2026-02-26](https://latent.space/p/wtf2025/)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-07-23](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-07-23-not-much/)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-06-29](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-06-29-not-much/))

## The evals group: Hamel Husain, Eugene Yan, Kapoor and Narayanan

Hamel Husain (94 findings) and Eugene Yan (48 findings) write on evals and testing, coding agents and agent orchestration. Husain observes that benchmarks are consumed like credit scores without disclaimers, so users misread their flaws. He also wants a web app where people read conversation traces and annotate them. Yan wants a tool that links eval harnesses to experiment pipelines so a model migration can be evaluated in one step. ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-11-05](https://latent.space/p/ainews-it-s-swapping-season/)) ([Hamel Husain, 2026-09-30](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/claude-auto-evals/)) ([Eugene Yan, 2025-11-23](https://eugeneyan.com//writing/product-evals/))

Sayash Kapoor and Arvind Narayanan (95 findings, AI as Normal Technology) press for more rigorous agent evaluation. They want shared best practices, leaderboards that show rising evaluation costs, and ways for real-world deployers to feed insights back to labs. Narayanan alone (49 findings) also wants reliability measured on consistency, robustness, calibration and operational safety, and he describes AI-generated spam as overwhelming communication channels. **Why follow:** they give you vocabulary to question headline benchmark numbers. ([AI as Normal Technology, 2026-10-19](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/new-paper-emerging-trends-in-ai-agent)) ([AI as Normal Technology, 2026-10-19](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/new-paper-emerging-trends-in-ai-agent)) ([AI as Normal Technology, 2026-10-19](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/new-paper-emerging-trends-in-ai-agent)) ([AI as Normal Technology, 2026-07-13](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/what-will-be-left-for-us-to-work)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-09-17](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-186-the-world-takes-notice))

## Frontier labs and policy: Jack Clark, Anthropic, Dean Ball, Timothy B. Lee

Jack Clark (69 findings, Import AI) and Anthropic (62 findings) focus on research, safety and evals. Clark wants third-party testing for self-improvement properties and automated labs where AI runs experiments. He has also estimated a 60%+ chance that automated AI R&D leads toward superintelligence by end of 2028. Anthropic's material asks for employee-like access for independent auditors, and notes that biological databases were not designed for agent use. **Treat these as lab-side views, and attribute them clearly.** ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-01-15](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-151-while-claude-coworks)) ([Import AI, 2026-10-19](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-477-in-which-i-tell-the)) ([The Algorithmic Bridge, 2026-05-19](https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/andrej-karpathy-joins-anthropic-what)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-10-27](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/anthropic-versus-anthropic)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-06-08](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-06-08-not-much/))

Dean W. Ball (47 findings) argues about governance. He wants independent verification infrastructure for frontier labs and compute futures markets to help startups hedge capacity risk. Timothy B. Lee (56 findings, Understanding AI) raises grounded risks, such as self-replicating agents that may be impossible to shut down, and tacit knowledge that is hard to capture. **Why follow:** they add policy and economic angles that technical blogs often skip. ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-09-08](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/astra-is-hard-to-monitor)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-10-29](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-192-anthropic-is-going-public)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-09-01](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/huggingface-attack-postmortem-civilizations)) ([Import AI, 2026-06-22](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-462-superpersuasion-self))
