What this means for you

1. Write about the verification gap, not the generation gap. The strongest builder pain is review and loss of understanding. As someone who builds with AI tools, you can add first-hand detail on what you check and how, such as separate reviewer models, tests first, or reading the spec instead of the code. Ben Tossell's point that specs may be easier to review than code is a good prompt. 2. Cost per task is a fresh, concrete angle. Per-token prices mislead, and observability & cost is rising. Build a small log of what your own projects cost per finished task across models, and post the comparison. It speaks to the cost confusion that Simon Willison and Tunguz describe. 1. Who is responsible when AI causes harm, and what exactly should be regulated?

The question: If an AI system or agent does damage, who pays, and should rules aim at the model, the chips, or the way it is used? This was the most-debated topic of the year. Don't Worry About the Vase · Sep 28, 2026Latent Space · Sep 17, 2026

How to read this

Each topic is measured as mentions per 1,000 findings, comparing December 2025 to March 2026 with July to October 2026. A topic is called rising or falling only when the verdict says so, which depends on how many of the larger sources moved the same way. That matters because the source mix shifted: AINews fell from 40.1% to 23.7% of findings, while Don't Worry About the Vase rose from 12.8% to 19.1% and Latent Space from 6.8% to 13.2%.

The window ends on 10 November 2026, so the last month is partial and its small numbers are noisy. Treat monthly zeros and jumps in November with care.

What this means for you

Write about the conditions of use, not the leaderboard. The data suggests the audience's attention moved from which tool is best to what could go wrong and what it costs: permissions, security, usage limits, access being revoked. A post explaining how you scoped what an AI agent could touch in your own project would fit where the conversation is heading.
