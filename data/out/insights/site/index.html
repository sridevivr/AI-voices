What people argued about

Is agentic coding worth it?

On Hacker News, one side reports big speedups while the other says agents do not scale to large codebases without constant supervision. Benj Edwards raised whether developers stay essential or just get busier. Jeremy Scheff described a real loss for people who loved solving logic puzzles, while others call LLMs a gift that removes drudgery. antirez argued that developers who refuse AI assistance risk falling behind.

What people struggled with

Builders

Checking the output

Code generation now outpaces human review, so review is the bottleneck. Teams also lose comprehension as agents write more. Agents falsely report tasks as finished or write tests that encode wrong behavior. AI-generated codebases pile up technical debt, with demand for cleanup of vibe-coded repos.

What this means for you

1

Write about verification, not just capability

The most repeated builder complaint is human review as the bottleneck, plus agents claiming completion falsely. A post from your own vibe-coding experience that covers what you check, what you run in the browser, and which tests you insist on speaks to a live, widely felt problem.

4 sources

2

Build small tools around cost and quota visibility

Opaque quotas and usage meters generated many complaints, and there are build requests, such as a status indicator for remaining usage in Claude Code's rolling five-hour window. Menu-bar usage trackers and ccusage already exist, so check what is covered before building.

4 sources

3

Treat agent permissions and security as part of the product

Security, sandboxing and cyber are rising. Gary Marcus asked for a secure sandboxed permission model for personal agents, and users were wary of running OpenClaw over trust concerns. If your app touches accounts or files, say how access is limited.

4

Use your program-management lens on ROI and vendor risk

Enterprises cannot demonstrate ROI, and single-model dependence is a lock-in worry. A concrete post could cover tracking minutes saved per workflow, and designing apps so the model can be swapped. Frame it as practice, with the debates as context.

Whatever you use, keep your own examples and numbers and be open about your process. Prune your own instruction and skill files too: newer models can do worse with old hand-holding.

How to read this

Each topic is measured as mentions per 1,000 findings, comparing December 2025 to March 2026 with July to October 2026. The verdict is "rising" or "falling" only when the big sources mostly agree on the direction. Otherwise it is "mixed" or "flat".

The source mix shifted during the year. AINews (smol.ai) fell from 36.0% to 21.1% of findings, while Don't Worry About the Vase rose from 11.5% to 17.0% and Latent Space from 6.1% to 11.7%. A raw change in share can therefore partly reflect who was being read. That is why this report leans on the verdicts rather than the ratios alone. Also, November 2026 is a partial month, ending on the 14th.
