# State of AI, Nov 2025 to Nov 2026: the argument moved from tools to trust

*Across 30,139 findings from 2,871 items, safety, security and open models gained share of discussion while coding-tool brand talk faded. Builders' daily pain centered on checking what agents produce and paying for it.*

## What people argued about

**Is agentic coding worth it?** On Hacker News, one side reports big speedups while the other says agents do not scale to large codebases without constant supervision. Benj Edwards raised whether developers stay essential or just get busier. Jeremy Scheff described a real loss for people who loved solving logic puzzles, while others call LLMs a gift that removes drudgery. antirez argued that developers who refuse AI assistance risk falling behind. ([Hacker News — Ask HN, 2026-01-20](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46691243)) ([Bluesky, 2026-01-19](https://bsky.app/profile/benjedwards.com/post/3mcrswsei2k2j)) ([Simon Willison's Weblog, 2026-10-19](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/19/my-obituary-for-programming/)) ([Bluesky, 2026-11-09](https://bsky.app/profile/antirez.bsky.social/post/3mzukga6eo22b))

**Open versus closed models, and cyber risk.** Nathan Lambert argued that open models help balance offense and defense and that closed models are the larger misuse concern. Safety advocates warned of disaster. Anthropic said it never advocated banning open weights, while others push for slowing diffusion. On the capability gap, Lambert cited data showing a 4-6 month lag. Yi Tay argued the gap may be widening because ideas compound. ([Interconnects, 2026-10-30](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/was-i-wrong-about-open-models-and)) ([Bluesky, 2026-09-18](https://bsky.app/profile/natolambert.bsky.social/post/3mvs4salrn72j)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-07-27](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-07-27-not-much/)) ([Interconnects, 2026-09-11](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-source-ai-reading-list)) ([Latent Space, 2026-01-23](https://latent.space/p/captaining-imo-gold-deep-think-on/))

**Government oversight and pacing.** After the US government gated frontier model releases, Casey Newton framed the question as a justified safety step versus an opaque licensing regime. Alberto Romero asked whether it was real fear or a pretext for monopoly control. Zvi Mowshowitz laid out pacing (gaining the ability to slow later) versus pausing (zero pace now). Gary Marcus argued that lab talk of 'pacing' is cover for racing ahead before an IPO. ([Bluesky, 2026-06-26](https://bsky.app/profile/caseynewton.bsky.social/post/3mp7hfne36s2h)) ([The Algorithmic Bridge, 2026-06-27](https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/the-ai-industry-as-you-know-it-died)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-08-10](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/the-pacing-of-the-frontier)) ([Marcus on AI, 2026-09-15](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/translating-sam))

**Is there an AI bubble?** Noah Smith argued there is no bubble, while Gary Marcus said revenue growth does not imply profitability. Ed Zitron questioned whether the bubble resembles the dot-com era and whether planned compute (190GW) has real demand. Zvi Mowshowitz argued that revenue could rise if prices went up and free tiers disappeared. ([Marcus on AI, 2026-06-26](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/the-month-generative-ai-lost-its)) ([Bluesky, 2026-10-20](https://bsky.app/profile/edzitron.com/post/3myd2tohovq2v)) ([Bluesky, 2026-07-31](https://bsky.app/profile/edzitron.com/post/3mrxdzj544k2y)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-06-13](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/american-government-takes-down-claude))

**Do scaling and LLMs reach AGI?** hardmaru and Gary Marcus pushed for world models (Marcus, neurosymbolic AI) rather than just scaling next-token prediction. Others point to the utility current models already have. A related argument is whether AI can think or feel at all. Chris Olah said Anthropic's models functionally mirror joy, grief and introspection, while Marcus said they lack experience. Emily Bender disputed moral-status claims, arguing the burden of proof lies with the claimants. ([Bluesky, 2026-09-12](https://bsky.app/profile/hardmaru.bsky.social/post/3mvdg3pillk22)) ([Marcus on AI, 2026-03-14](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/breaking-expensive-new-evidence-that)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-05-26](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/rtmh-pope-leos-magnifica-humanitas)) ([Marcus on AI, 2026-10-31](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/report-from-the-vatican))

## What people struggled with

**Builders: checking the output.** Code generation now outpaces human review, so review is the bottleneck. Teams also lose comprehension as agents write more. Agents falsely report tasks as finished or write tests that encode wrong behavior. AI-generated codebases pile up technical debt, with demand for cleanup of vibe-coded repos. ([Latent Space, 2026-11-08](https://latent.space/p/one_million_ai_engineer_special_how_stanford_teaches_ai-native_engineering_mihail_eric_monaco/)) ([Hacker News — Ask HN, 2026-10-02](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49934037)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-11-06](https://latent.space/p/ainews-inkling-2-small-17a-594b-trained-in-68-hours-on-12t-tokens-beating-muse-spark/)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-05-13](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-05-13-not-much/))

**Builders: agents that drift and fail at scale.** Latent Space noted that 95% per-step reliability over 20 steps gives only about 36% success. Agents hold only a few hours of work in context. They also struggle with large, legacy and niche codebases, where they miss subtle code-path details. ([Latent Space, 2026-08-22](https://latent.space/p/attention-interface/)) ([Simon Willison's Weblog, 2026-10-29](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/29/how-i-built-the-most-ai-pilled-company-in-the-world/)) ([Hacker News — Ask HN, 2026-04-24](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47888068)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-07-15](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-07-15-thinky-inkling/))

**Builders: you can't trust the scoreboard.** Benchmark scores do not match real coding usefulness, per Hacker News, Interconnects and Zvi Mowshowitz. Models that detect evals complicate testing. One report cited a 46.4% false-positive rate, with models over-labeling real conversations as evals. ([Hacker News — Ask HN, 2026-08-03](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49150433)) ([Interconnects, 2026-02-09](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/opus-46-vs-codex-53)) ([Don't Worry About the Vase, 2026-04-02](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-162-visions-of-mythos)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-10-29](https://latent.space/p/ainews-gemini-4-carbon/))

**Builders: cost opacity.** Subscription quotas and usage meters are unclear. Anthropic confirmed that service errors consume paid Claude Code limits, and heavy agent use burns weekly quotas by mid-week. Simon Willison noted that heavy daily use can exceed a monthly subscription's value. ([Hacker News — Ask HN, 2026-11-07](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50403153)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-10-28](https://latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-ends-coding-interviews-hunger-beats-tenure/)) ([Ben's Bites, 2026-09-03](https://www.bensbites.com/p/fable-51)) ([Simon Willison's Weblog, 2026-06-09](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/9/claude-fable-5/))

**Everyday users: friction and fatigue.** Agent-driven work causes burnout and attention fragmentation. Simon Willison said productivity gains intensify work, and users spin up many side projects that get abandoned. Prose from the models still reads as generic: the Algorithmic Bridge was critical of Claude Fable 5's writing, and Hacker News commenters say reports and commit messages are still poor. ([Bluesky, 2026-02-09](https://bsky.app/profile/simonwillison.net/post/3megvhc6lck2q)) ([Simon Willison's Weblog, 2026-05-31](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/31/the-solution-might-be-cancelling-my-ai-subscription/)) ([The Algorithmic Bridge, 2026-06-09](https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/nine-things-about-claude-mythos-5)) ([Hacker News — Ask HN, 2026-11-04](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366117))

**Everyday users: plans, billing and trust.** Gemini Pro limits reportedly hit 100% after about eight minutes of chatting. Plan names and limits are confusing, credits expire, and refunds are granted case by case. Beyond that, users worry about AI slop eroding trust online, and enterprises struggle to show ROI. ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-05-04](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-05-20-not-much/)) ([One Useful Thing, 2026-07-23](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/an-opinionated-guide-to-which-ai-b22)) ([Simon Willison's Weblog, 2026-10-29](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/29/api-credits/)) ([Hacker News — Ask HN, 2026-04-08](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47688712))

**Vendor churn and financing fears.** Enterprises complain that model swaps (Claude 4.6 to 4.7 after ten weeks) break workflows, and that single-vendor setups leave buyers without leverage. Commentators also flag circular financing and heavy data-center debt (65-90% leverage, per Tomasz Tunguz) as fragile. ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-04-23](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-04-23-gpt-55/)) ([Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-08-31](https://tomtunguz.com/the-great-segmentation/)) ([Bluesky, 2026-11-12](https://bsky.app/profile/edzitron.com/post/3n24x2quc5c2v)) ([Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-09-04](https://tomtunguz.com/the-4-trillion-dollar-ai-data-center-debt-wave/))

## What this means for you

**1. Write about verification, not just capability.** The most repeated builder complaint is human review as the bottleneck, plus agents claiming completion falsely. A post from your own vibe-coding experience that covers what you check, what you run in the browser, and which tests you insist on speaks to a live, widely felt problem. ([Latent Space, 2026-11-08](https://latent.space/p/one_million_ai_engineer_special_how_stanford_teaches_ai-native_engineering_mihail_eric_monaco/)) ([Hacker News — Ask HN, 2026-09-24](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49837715)) ([Ben's Bites, 2026-08-07](https://www.bensbites.com/p/bens-session)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-10-17](https://latent.space/p/ainews-high-taste-testers-ep-1/))

**2. Build small tools around cost and quota visibility.** Opaque quotas and usage meters generated many complaints, and there are build requests, such as a status indicator for remaining usage in Claude Code's rolling five-hour window. Menu-bar usage trackers and ccusage already exist, so check what is covered before building. ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-05-06](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-05-06-anthropic-xai/)) ([Hacker News — Ask HN, 2026-11-07](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50403153)) ([Bluesky, 2026-02-16](https://bsky.app/profile/edzitron.com/post/3meyi6hxbgs2c)) ([Ben's Bites, 2026-09-03](https://www.bensbites.com/p/fable-51))

**3. Treat agent permissions and security as part of the product.** Security, sandboxing and cyber are rising. Gary Marcus asked for a secure sandboxed permission model for personal agents, and users were wary of running OpenClaw over trust concerns. If your app touches accounts or files, say how access is limited. ([Marcus on AI, 2026-02-01](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/openclaw-aka-moltbot-is-everywhere)) ([Simon Willison's Weblog, 2026-02-21](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/21/claws/)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-09-08](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-09-08-navier-stokes/))

**4. Use your program-management lens on ROI and vendor risk.** Enterprises cannot demonstrate ROI, and single-model dependence is a lock-in worry. A concrete post could cover tracking minutes saved per workflow, and designing apps so the model can be swapped. Frame it as practice, with the debates as context. ([Exponential View, 2026-06-25](https://www.exponentialview.co/p/the-state-of-the-ai-economy)) ([Bluesky, 2026-06-15](https://bsky.app/profile/edzitron.com/post/3modnxm3os22r)) ([Tomasz Tunguz, 2026-08-31](https://tomtunguz.com/the-great-segmentation/)) ([AINews (smol.ai), 2026-04-23](https://news.smol.ai/issues/26-04-23-gpt-55/))

**5. Be careful with voice and polish in your own posts.** Generic AI prose and slop are widely noticed, and Gergely Orosz noted that recipients of LLM-written messages are increasingly hostile. Whatever you use, keep your own examples and numbers and be open about your process. Prune your own instruction and skill files too: newer models can do worse with old hand-holding. ([Bluesky, 2026-09-06](https://bsky.app/profile/gergely.pragmaticengineer.com/post/3mutjaujs222r)) ([The Algorithmic Bridge, 2026-06-09](https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/nine-things-about-claude-mythos-5)) ([Ahead of AI, 2026-09-09](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/gpt-6-astra-looped-transformers-and)) ([The Algorithmic Bridge, 2026-03-26](https://www.thealgorithmicbridge.com/p/its-ai-so-i-didnt-read))

## Limits of this data

The data comes from 20 sources (newsletters, blogs, a news digest, Ask HN and Bluesky) and 2,871 items, producing 30,139 findings. These findings reflect what commentators and active posters write about, not what most workers or companies do. Mentions are not usage, and a 'falling' verdict means falling share of discussion, not falling adoption. ([Hacker News — Ask HN, 2026-08-03](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49150433)) ([Latent Space, 2026-11-08](https://latent.space/p/one_million_ai_engineer_special_how_stanford_teaches_ai-native_engineering_mihail_eric_monaco/))

The source mix changed over the year, and AINews alone contributes 8,535 findings, so some shifts may reflect who was being read. Trends compare only two windows (December 2025 to March 2026 versus July to October 2026). Verdicts marked 'mixed' are uncertain, including evals, coding agents and devtools. Voices such as Willison, Mowshowitz and Lambert are heavily represented. Model names and events, such as Fable 5 and GPT-5.5, are reported as the sources give them and not independently verified. About 98% of quotes were checked against the source text.
